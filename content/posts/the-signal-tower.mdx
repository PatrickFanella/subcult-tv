# The signal tower: why every collective needs a place to broadcast from

Open subcult.tv and the work sits together: software, media, essays, active projects, and experiments still incubating.

That arrangement is deliberate. On a social platform, each item arrives alone. A launch note can draw attention for a day, then sink beneath newer posts. Finding it later depends on a search system we do not control.

Here, a visitor can move from a project to its repository or find an older argument in the zine. Those links keep context that is usually absent from a timeline.

We call this site a signal tower. It gives the collective a stable address and a view of its own work.

Subcult.tv does not replace social networks or guarantee discovery. We still publish into systems built around circulation. The site gives those fragments somewhere to point back to.

That address also limits what a platform can define for us. A profile assembled from recent posts reflects whatever the collective published last week. An archive shows commitments that recur over years.

The catalog puts self-hosted services beside small tools, cultural work, and experiments in non-extractive measurement. Some projects run in public. Others remain incubating. Their proximity matters more than tidy categories.

The catalog should not become a productivity display. Its useful job is to expose connections. A transcript tool and a zine both care about memory. A community graph and a mutual-aid service both force questions about trust and power.

> A collective needs a place where the work can be encountered as a body, not a feed.

Publishing those connections is a governance choice. Routes, status labels, project histories, and repository links tell visitors how the collective operates. They are more revealing than a polished paragraph on an about page.

The politics are present in the design. A zine makes room for arguments longer than a post. The archive keeps earlier work available. Repository links provide public evidence. An optional newsletter signup sits in the footer and does not use tracking.

This does not produce perfect coherence. A collective contains disagreements, abandoned paths, and work at different stages. The site has to make that uneven record navigable without sanding it into a brand story.

The internet has plenty of launch pages. We wanted a place where an idea could remain available after launch day, still attached to the work around it.

You can leave the feed and still find us.
