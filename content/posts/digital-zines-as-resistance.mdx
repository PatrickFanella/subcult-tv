# Digital zines as resistance

Before the feed decided what surfaced next, people made zines.

They were handmade, opinionated, and sometimes rough around the edges. They passed from person to person and gathered small groups of readers around a scene or an idea. Nobody needed an engagement strategy to make one worth reading.

---

A feed can move a post quickly, then bury it just as fast. Writing arrives out of context, squeezed between ads, arguments, and whatever the platform wants to promote next.

A zine asks for a different kind of attention. Pieces sit together on purpose. The reader can follow a thought across pages, close the issue, and find the same arrangement later.

Digital publishing makes zines easier to produce and share. Still, much of the writing we find online follows the feed's pace: quick posts, constant reaction, little reason to return. Short work is fine. Disposable work is the problem.

Platforms reward what gets an immediate response. Yesterday's useful thought disappears under today's fight, and the platform keeps the archive on terms it can change whenever it likes.

A digital zine lets an editor say, "These pieces belong together, and we want them to stick around."

> Preservation is political.

Platform policy changes can hide old work or make it harder to reach. A publication with archived essays and curated collections gives its makers more control over what remains available.

---

At Subculture Collective, long-form writing helps us keep a record of what we are doing and why.

An essay can pin down a decision or explain a value well enough for a future contributor to challenge it. Without a shared record, we end up relying on half-remembered conversations and posts scattered across other people's platforms.

Digital tools are part of the work. We choose to use them to assemble pieces with context, rather than chase a spike of attention and hand an algorithm the final say.

Self-publishing preserves choices that a feed strips away: sequence, context, and the terms of access. A small archive will not defeat platform power. It can keep a piece of culture from being entirely dependent on it.

Reach is useful, but it is a poor measure of what deserves to last.

A zine, digital or physical, keeps related work in one place. That modest act gives readers a way back and gives makers a memory they control.
