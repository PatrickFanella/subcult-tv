# Moderation is infrastructure

We tend to notice moderation only after something has gone wrong. A rule appears, someone gets banned, a thread closes. By then the harder work has already been missed, because moderation belongs in the design of a community from the start.

Every system assumes people will behave in certain ways. Some leave openings for abuse. Others put so many locks on participation that nobody wants to stay. Good moderation plans for conflict without letting conflict run the place.

Coordinated disruption can look boring at first: a burst of junk submissions, a cluster of votes arriving together, someone testing the same boundary over and over. Each action is easy to dismiss. The pattern is what damages trust.

Waiting for a volunteer to catch every incident is a bad system. People get tired. Similar cases get different outcomes. The loudest problem takes the day while quieter damage piles up. Some of that load can move to mechanisms. Rate limits can slow coordinated manipulation. Trust built through participation can affect how much weight an action carries. Public decision logs give people something firmer than rumor.

None of that removes judgment. Rules cannot anticipate every conflict, and unlimited discretion makes enforcement feel personal. Clear norms give moderators a basis for acting and members a basis for questioning a decision. Arbitrary enforcement teaches people to avoid the moderator rather than trust the process. Written reasons and visible principles open decisions to scrutiny, and shared procedures keep the whole job off one person's shoulders.

When this goes well it is hard to see. That does not mean the community runs itself. Someone wrote the rules, kept records, noticed patterns, and made room for an appeal.

Moderation decisions show what a community will protect and what it will tolerate. For SUBCULT those choices matter at least as much as another feature release. Treat moderation as an afterthought and people absorb harm that could have been prevented. Design it in and disagreement has somewhere to go besides a pile-on, a private feud, or an exhausted volunteer's inbox.
