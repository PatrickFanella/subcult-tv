Do not compress missed posts into the weekend.

Keep two or three 15-minute human participation windows on publishing days, as capacity permits. Replies and relationship work stay outside the automated dispatcher.

Prepare one Patreon field note a week and one studio ledger a month. Publish the ledger in place of that week's field note when that avoids duplicate production. A Patreon promotion points to a verified live destination and appears at most once per three or four useful public posts.

## Source selection and bank

Use one anchor source each week. CLPR may have its own product source; do not force an unrelated product story into the studio theme. Rotate Proof, Machinery, Consequence and Memory over four weeks, starting at the first full pilot week. Themes guide selection. They do not override readiness.

Build the candidate list from current work and existing idea banks. Rank at most five candidates by current truth, audience fit, usefulness, distinct account angle, available proof, preparation effort and the user's interest. Keep a small reserve of approved evergreen posts for quiet weeks. Reuse an entry only when its account-specific reuse policy permits it.

The bank has four editorial states: `candidate`, `draft`, `approved` and `retired`. Dispatch records separately track `scheduled`, `published`, `failed` or `needs_reconciliation`. An idea, a completed task or an old launch draft is never automatic approval to publish.

By default, approval covers one scheduled use of the exact copy, media, account and publication window. Evergreen reuse requires an explicit reuse interval and expiration rule. With no candidate, the slot stays empty, unless an eligible approved reserve post fills it.

## Pilot reviews

At four weeks, review published URLs against the standard and the floor, along with substantive conversation, useful questions, relevant attention, supporter or work opportunities, preparation time and bank health. Treat raw impressions as context. Keep, reduce or expand one lane at a time, based on repeated results and not on one post that did unusually well or badly.

[[Publishing History and Cleanup]] lists the retired campaigns. Recommission a candidate explicitly when it still matters. Do not restart an expired week or carry old due dates into the pilot.

## Proposed job behavior

1. On Monday, read this plan, the bank, recent source work, relevant Super Productivity tasks and Buffer queues. Maintain one weekly note and reuse existing tasks instead of duplicating their commitments.
2. Select at most five candidates with a real source, supportable claim, audience and preparation cost. Write the chosen account-specific drafts into the weekly note.
3. Queue only the exact account-specific versions explicitly marked approved for automatic scheduling. Approval includes copy, media, destination and publication window; changing any of those requires a new approval.
4. Use explicit future dates when the editorial plan requires a day that differs from the channel's queue slots. Otherwise use the existing Buffer queue. Do not silently change account schedules.
5. Record content ID, account ID, approved revision hash, intended slot, Buffer post ID and delivery state in SQLite. Serialize dispatch and keep a unique key for the content/account/slot combination.
6. Reconcile ambiguous submissions before retrying. If the publisher response cannot be matched confidently, flag it for review; do not create another post.
7. On a daily check, reconcile delivery, record the published URL or error, and notify only on failures or required action. A scheduled ID proves scheduling; a public URL proves publication.
8. Keep production, approval, scheduling and publication as separate task states. Complete a publish task only with a public URL or an explicit editorial skip.

No bank entry means an empty slot, not generated filler. Expired campaigns do not become posting debt. Participation and replies remain human work.

## Content-entry contract

Each publishable entry needs a stable ID, exact destination channel, final copy, optional final media and alt text, source links, evidence date, intended local publication window, expiration/reuse policy, and approval for that exact version. The job computes and stores the approved revision hash.

Keep editorial notes and original media in Ars Malefica or their existing library. SQLite owns dispatch and delivery state. Buffer Ideas can mirror selected drafts later, but there must not be two competing content-bank masters.
