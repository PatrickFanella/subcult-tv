# Reference captions

These show the register we want. Each lists its evidence, from a change or the claims register. They teach voice and structure. Never reuse their facts, numbers or wording in a new post: a new post uses only its own evidence.

## 1. The promise the app couldn't keep (subcult, Subcult OS)

> "Check your email" is a promise. Subcult OS was making it without knowing.
>
> Ask for a password reset and the app told you the link was on its way. It had no idea. The server only knows it took the request.
>
> So now it says what it actually knows: we got your request, look for a link.
>
> Subcult OS is the software we're building for running free shows: the list, the door, close-out. Still in development.

Evidence: the recovery notice said a link had been sent; the API provides no delivery confirmation; both notices now leave delivery unconfirmed.

Why it works: the first line names a familiar phrase and makes it strange. The story is one small honesty fix, told from the reader's side. The product line comes after the reader cares. Maturity is three words at the end.

## 2. The boring part (patrick, Undertow)

> Open a new project while the last one is still saving, and Undertow could quietly send your next edits into the old one.
>
> Autosave is supposed to be the boring part. Saves now finish in order, and a late reply from a project you've closed gets ignored.
>
> Undertow turns a track into a visualizer video in your browser. This fix isn't on the live editor yet.

Evidence: an autosave finishing after another project opened could redirect edits into the old project; autosaves are now serialized and results from previously opened documents are ignored.

Why it works: second person puts the reader in the moment of the bug. "Autosave is supposed to be the boring part" is a dry aside that is true without claiming anything. One mechanism sentence, in plain words. (Patrick owns a bug outright only when the evidence shows his earlier change caused it, as with a removed link that left a broken button behind.)

## 3. Same instant, two labels (subcult, Subcult OS)

> A show in Tokyo and a show in Chicago could both say 3:00 PM. Same instant, two labels, one of them wrong.
>
> Event times now use the event's own time zone. 3:00 PM in Chicago reads 5:00 AM the next day in Tokyo, and every card shows its offset.
>
> Run sheets live and die by the clock. Subcult OS is in development.

Evidence: the same instant displayed 3:00 PM with both Chicago and Tokyo labels; it now shows Chicago 3:00 PM and Tokyo 5:00 AM the next day; cards include the offset.

Why it works: the exact numbers are the hook. The fix is shown, not described. "Run sheets live and die by the clock" carries the product's run-sheet register without claiming anything.

## 4. Start with the creator (subcult, clpr)

> You remember the Twitch streamer. The clip title, less so.
>
> The link could be halfway up an old group chat by now. You just want to watch that one bit again.
>
> We built clpr so you can browse Twitch clips by creator.
>
> At clpr.tv, start with the creator you remember.

Evidence: C-CLPR supports browsing Twitch clips by creator at clpr.tv. The owner approved this wording on October 19, 2026, changing only line endings. The group chat is a possible reader situation, not a reported incident or a promise that clpr has the clip.

Why it works: the remembered creator supplies both the hook and a concrete next step, with one supported capability between them.

## 5. The product, not the menu (subcult, Undertow)

> Your track is finished. Undertow turns it into a visualizer video.
>
> We built it as a layered music visualizer editor, with six templates to start from.
>
> The public editor runs in your browser. You work on the video’s layers and export an MP4 on your own machine.

Evidence: the claims register's Undertow row (layered editor, six templates, browser rendering, local MP4 export). The owner approved this caption on October 10, 2026, and rejected one from the same product the next day that opened "Adding a visual layer in Undertow could open a menu taller than its own panel" and went on to measure the menu in pixels.

Why it works: the first line is the reader's moment. Every sentence after it is something the reader gets. There is no bug, no before, and the maturity is carried by "public editor". The rejected caption was accurate and still worthless to a stranger, because its subject was a repair.

## 6. Before and after the same caption

Before (changelog): "We're working on how Patchwork shows the neighborhood. The revised home replaces an explainer card with an excerpt of nearby places and open requests when any exist. These interface changes are merged, with a live check still due."

After: "Open Patchwork and the first thing you should see is your neighbors, not a card explaining the idea. The home page now leads with nearby places and open requests. It's still a demo, so please don't arrange real help through it yet. Requests show approximate areas; only approved public resources show an address."

What changed: the reader's experience replaced the diff; review words disappeared; the demo warning and location rule stayed, in plain words.

## 7. One address (subcult, Kork)

> Someone asks where to hear your music and when you’re playing next. Give them one address.
>
> We built Kork as a free link page where your music player can sit beside your tour dates.
>
> Kork is still early, with rough edges. You can build right away; new accounts wait 24 hours before publishing.
>
> Start your page at kork.subcult.tv.

Evidence: C-KORK supports free link pages, music embeds, events and tour dates, early status and the 24-hour publishing wait. The owner approved this wording on October 15, 2026, changing only line endings.

Why it works: a familiar request leads directly to a supported use, with the early status and publishing wait kept brief.
