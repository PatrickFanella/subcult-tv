## Source selection and bank

Use one anchor source object each week. CLPR may have one separate product source; do not force an unrelated product story into the studio theme. Rotate Proof, Machinery, Consequence, and Memory over four weeks, beginning at the first full pilot week. Themes guide selection; they do not override readiness.

Build the candidate list from current work and existing idea banks. Rank at most five candidates by current truth, audience fit, usefulness, distinct account angle, available proof, preparation effort, and the user's interest. Keep a small reserve of approved evergreen posts for quiet weeks. Reuse only when an entry's account-specific reuse policy permits it.

The bank has four editorial states: `candidate`, `draft`, `approved`, and `retired`. Dispatch records separately track `scheduled`, `published`, `failed`, or `needs_reconciliation`. An idea, task completion, or old launch draft is never automatic approval to publish.

By default, approval covers one scheduled use of the exact copy, media, account, and publication window. Evergreen reuse requires an explicit reuse interval and expiration rule. No candidate means an empty slot; a reusable approved reserve may fill it if eligible.

## Pilot reviews

At four weeks, review published URLs against the standard and floor, substantive conversation, useful questions, relevant attention, supporter or work opportunities, preparation time, and bank health. Treat raw impressions as context. Keep, reduce, or expand one lane at a time based on repeated results rather than one unusually successful or unsuccessful post.

Use [[Publishing History and Cleanup]] for the retired campaigns. Recommission a candidate explicitly when it still matters; do not restart an expired week or carry old due dates into the pilot.

## Proposed job behavior

1. On Monday, read this governing plan, the bank, recent source work, relevant Super Productivity tasks, and Buffer queues. Maintain one weekly note and reuse existing tasks rather than duplicating their commitments.
2. Select at most five candidates with a real source, supportable claim, audience, and preparation cost. Produce the chosen account-specific drafts in one weekly note.
3. Queue only the exact account-specific versions explicitly marked approved for automatic scheduling. Approval includes copy, media, destination, and publication window; changing any of those requires a new approval.
4. Use explicit future dates when the editorial plan requires a day that differs from the channel's queue slots. Otherwise use the existing Buffer queue. Do not silently change account schedules.
5. Record content ID, account ID, approved revision hash, intended slot, Buffer post ID, and delivery state in SQLite. Serialize dispatch and keep a unique key for the content/account/slot combination.
6. Reconcile ambiguous submissions before retrying. If the publisher response cannot be matched confidently, flag it for review rather than create another post.
7. On a daily check, reconcile delivery, record the published URL or error, and notify only on failures or required action. A scheduled ID proves scheduling; a public URL proves publication.
8. Keep production, approval, scheduling, and publication as separate task states. Complete a publish task only with a public URL or an explicit editorial skip.

No bank entry means an empty slot, not generated filler. Expired campaigns do not become posting debt. Participation and replies remain human work under this editorial plan.

## Content-entry contract

Each publishable entry needs a stable ID, exact destination channel, final copy, optional final media and alt text, source links, evidence date, intended local publication window, expiration/reuse policy, and approval for that exact version. The job computes and stores the approved revision hash.

Keep editorial notes and original media in Ars Malefica or their existing library. SQLite owns dispatch and delivery state. Buffer Ideas can mirror selected drafts later, but do not make two competing content-bank masters.
