# New SUBCULT Bumps: The Fair at Dusk

I went to the fair at dusk with a phone and came back with a small set of visual fragments for SUBCULT.

The rides were already doing most of the work: saturated lights, metal structures against the evening sky, the ferris wheel, a cross-shaped silhouette, and a few moments that felt halfway between a local fair and a transmission from somewhere else.

I’ve cut those fragments into a set of short bump videos for `subcult.tv`:

- `subcult-ferris.mp4`
- `subcult-ufo.mp4`
- `subcult-cross.mp4`
- `subcult-cross-shadow.mp4`
- `subcult-collage.mp4`
- `subcult-top-secret.mp4`

The visual layer is mostly there. The next pass is sound.

I want to add music to these rather than treat them as silent loops. The main question is how much the music should explain the images. One direction is minimal and atmospheric: a short signal, drone, or pulse that lets the fair footage stay strange. Another is more rhythmic and synthetic, pushing the clips closer to title sequences or broadcast bumps.

My current leaning is to keep the tracks short, tactile, and slightly unresolved. The footage already has plenty of color and motion, so the music should give it a shared identity without turning every clip into the same piece.

This is the kind of small experiment I want to keep documenting here: a real place, a few minutes of footage, and the decisions that turn it into part of the SUBCULT world.

Next: score the first two clips, compare the tone, then carry the strongest musical rule across the rest of the set.

**Question for patrons:** should the music lean more toward eerie late-night transmission or bright synthetic fairground energy?

Follow along while I build the sound layer.
