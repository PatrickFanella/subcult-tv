# SUBCULT funding and partnership policy

> [!abstract] SUBCULT may use patron support, grants, sponsorships, paid partnerships, earned revenue, and aligned investment. No route is legitimate or illegitimate in itself. The terms decide whether a relationship adds to the work’s autonomy or compromises it.

> [!warning] Operating policy, not legal advice
> Equity, debt, securities, sponsorship, grant, tax, and intellectual-property agreements require appropriate legal and financial review before acceptance.

## Principle

Money pays for time, hosting, tools, design, research, accessibility and documentation, and it keeps an experiment running long enough to learn from it.

Money also brings control. Every funding relationship carries incentives, reporting demands, expectations or claims on future work. SUBCULT should state those openly before they start steering decisions unannounced.

## Public routes

| Route | Exchange | Does not automatically confer |
|---|---|---|
| **Support** | Recurring or one-time patronage; published tier benefits | Ownership, repayment, governance, guaranteed releases, or product control |
| **Grant** | Funding for stated public-interest work and agreed reporting | Ownership, exclusivity, or editorial control beyond the written grant |
| **Sponsorship** | Money or in-kind support for a named artifact, event, or period | Endorsement, access to user data, hidden placement, or control of conclusions |
| **Partnership** | Scoped collaboration with mutual deliverables | Umbrella-wide authority, indefinite exclusivity, or rights outside the agreement |
| **Investment** | Capital exchanged under negotiated financial and governance terms | Moral authority, community representation, access to private user data, or control beyond the signed instrument |
| **Earned revenue** | Payment for products, services, licenses, or commissions | Rights over unrelated SUBCULT projects or future canon |

## Support route

Patronage supports the workshop. Benefits may include process notes, early previews, build logs, drafts, office hours or advisory polls, when a tier lists them explicitly.

Supporters are not investors, and support does not buy governance. A poll is advisory unless the prompt states its exact scope, eligible voters, decision rule, and binding effect. A tier should promise only benefits the current operation can reliably deliver.

## Partner route

The public **Invest** route should become **Partner**. It may receive inquiries concerning:

- project collaborations;
- grants and public-interest funding;
- aligned sponsorships;
- infrastructure or distribution partnerships;
- commissioned work;
- licensing or service arrangements;
- carefully structured investment.

The route starts a conversation. It does not mean SUBCULT is always fundraising or that every kind of relationship is on offer.

## Boundaries that are not negotiable

SUBCULT should reject or redesign an arrangement that requires:

- undisclosed influence over editorial, research, moderation, or project status;
- sale of personal data, attention, or behavioral access;
- dark patterns, artificial engagement, or surveillance as a success condition;
- false claims about community ownership, participation, privacy, openness, or readiness;
- broad intellectual-property assignment unrelated to the funded deliverable;
- indefinite exclusivity or dependency that prevents a practical exit;
- suppression of security, safety, or material-conflict disclosures;
- a partner speaking for communities they do not represent;
- terms that cannot be explained honestly on the public record.

## Review gate

Before accepting material support or signing a partnership, record:

1. **Purpose:** What will the money or relationship make possible?
2. **Counterparty:** Who is involved, and what incentives do they carry?
3. **Exchange:** What does each side give, receive, own, and control?
4. **Data:** What information becomes accessible, retained, or reportable?
5. **Rights:** Are there licenses, exclusivity, approval, governance, or repayment terms?
6. **Exit:** How can either side end the arrangement, and what survives termination?
7. **Conflict:** Which SUBCULT principle is under the most pressure?
8. **Disclosure:** What should participants and the public be told?
9. **Authority:** Who may approve the agreement?
10. **Review:** Which legal, tax, security, or financial specialist must examine it?

A public inquiry form, a patron tier, a deck or someone's spoken enthusiasm does not replace this review.

## Transparency ledger

Record each material relationship in detail proportionate to its effect.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Counterparty | Named organization or individual unless confidentiality lawfully prevents it |
| Route | Support, grant, sponsorship, partnership, investment, or earned revenue |
| Purpose | Project, infrastructure, research, or general operations |
| Value | Exact amount or a meaningful public band when appropriate |
| Restrictions | Deliverables, reporting, deadlines, licenses, approval, or exclusivity |
| Control | Ownership, board, veto, governance, or product rights—state “none” when none |
| Data access | Data categories and retention, or “none” |
| Disclosure | Public, delayed, confidential with reason, or not material |
| Start / end | Effective period and exit terms |
| Status | Proposed, active, completed, declined, or terminated |

Small individual patron payments may be aggregated to protect privacy. Material institutional support that could reasonably affect trust must be listed on its own, not folded into an aggregate total.

## Public funding statement

> We may fund SUBCULT through patron support, earned revenue, grants, sponsorships, partnerships and aligned investment. We judge every arrangement by its terms: what it funds, what it controls and what it can see. If we cannot explain a deal in public without lying about the work, we change it or turn it down.

This is draft wording and no route in it is a statement of money received. Before publishing, name only the routes that have actually supplied funds (see [[001 Projects/Works/Social Posts/SUBCULT Marketing/one-sheet-subcult|the one-sheet]]). The wording follows the support voice in the [voice guide](../../VOICE.md).

Short version:

> We use several funding routes. None receives hidden control by default.

## Disclosure language

For sponsored or funded artifacts:

> This work received support from **[counterparty]** through **[route]**. The agreement covered **[scope]**. The counterparty **[did / did not]** receive editorial approval, product control, data access, or ownership rights. Full terms or a summary are available at **[link]**.

## Implementation decisions

- Navigation label: **Partner**, not **Invest**.
- Preferred future path: `/partner`; keep `/invest` only as a temporary redirect.
- Contact language: **Start a partnership inquiry**, not **Email investors**.
- Decks and one-pagers: label as partnership briefs; remove unverified traction, roadmap, market, and active-project claims.
- Support page: describe tier benefits and limits; do not frame supporters as members of governance.
- Metrics: publish dates and definitions; never present stale metrics as current transparency.
