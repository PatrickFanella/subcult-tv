# Patreon Page Copy

> [!abstract] Public Patreon copy for the Support route. Patreon funds the workshop and comes with the benefits named on each tier. It does not sell ownership, governance, guaranteed releases, or control of the roadmap.

## Page headline

# Who pays for the workshop

Most of what SUBCULT makes stays public. Patreon is where the people who use it can pay toward it, and where we post the working notes: build logs, early previews, drafts, experiments, and the reasoning behind our decisions.

## Short bio

SUBCULT builds open, inspectable software, media systems, research tools, and participatory experiments.

The finished work is in the public archive, and following it costs nothing. Patreon carries the process behind it. The money goes toward independent development, documentation, and publishing.

## Full About copy

SUBCULT is an underground creative-technical collective. We build software, media systems and research tools, and we publish them in the open.

The public side holds the projects, releases, zine, repositories, and archive. On Patreon we show the workshop: build logs, planning notes, early previews, drafts, technical decisions, experiments that failed, and now and then a question about one specific project choice. Answers to those questions are advisory.

Support helps pay for hosting, tools, research, design, accessibility, documentation, and production time. It does not buy ownership, governance, a guaranteed release schedule, or control over unrelated projects.

Following is free, and the public work stays public. Pay for a tier if the process is useful to you and you want to help keep it going.

## CTA block

> Pay toward the work and read how it gets made.

- Following is free: public updates and occasional previews.
- A paid tier gets exactly what that tier lists.
- Cancel when it stops being useful to you.

## Relaunch membership architecture

Start with free following and one paid tier. Add another paid tier only after at least four weeks of delivery and member response show a distinct, sustainable benefit.

### Free

For following the public work.

- Public updates and occasional previews.
- Invitations to read, inspect, test, or respond when a Work has a concrete participation path.

### Backer — $10/month

Ten dollars a month toward the workshop, with the working notes in return.

- One substantial field note each week.
- One studio ledger each month: what shipped, what changed, what it cost, what failed, and what comes next.
- Selected early previews, drafts, implementation notes, and research trails when they exist.
- Occasional advisory questions. Each one states its scope and effect before you answer.
- Access to the supporter archive.

Backer does not promise daily output, unlimited access, private consulting, governance rights, or that every roadmap item will ship.

## Future-tier gate

Possible future tiers such as Workshop or Underwriter are design options, not launch commitments. Before publishing one, define its added benefit, delivery cost, rights boundary, and exit condition.

## Benefit rules

- Replace "occasional chances to shape what ships next" with **advisory input on explicitly bounded choices**.
- A poll is nonbinding unless it names the eligible voters, decision rule, scope, and binding effect.
- "Early access" means access before a public release when a releasable artifact exists. It is not a release guarantee.
- "Closer line" means a defined group channel, office hour, or feedback thread. It does not mean unlimited direct access.
- Never promise daily or monthly output unless the operating cadence can sustain it.
- High-dollar tiers fund the operation. They do not become consulting retainers or governance seats.

## Funding transparency block

Patreon may not be our only source of money. SUBCULT may also use earned revenue, grants, sponsorships, partnerships, or aligned investment. What you pay on Patreon is patronage. It helps fund the work and comes with the listed benefits. It is not an investment, and it carries no expectation of repayment or ownership.
