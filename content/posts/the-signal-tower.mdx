# The signal tower: why every collective needs a place to broadcast from

Open subcult.tv and the work sits together: software, media, essays, active projects, and experiments still incubating.

That arrangement is deliberate. On a social platform each item arrives alone. A launch note draws attention for a day, then sinks beneath newer posts, and finding it later depends on a search system we do not control. Here a visitor can move from a project to its repository, or find an older argument in the zine. Those links keep the context a timeline drops.

We call the site a signal tower. It gives the collective a stable address and a view of its own work.

It does not replace social networks or guarantee discovery. We still publish into systems built around circulation, and the site gives those fragments somewhere to point back to. The address also limits what a platform can define for us. A profile assembled from recent posts reflects whatever the collective published last week. An archive shows the commitments that recur over years.

The catalog puts self-hosted services beside small tools, cultural work, and experiments in non-extractive measurement. Some projects run in public. Others are still incubating. We care more about what sits next to what than about tidy categories, and we do not want the catalog to become a productivity display. Its job is to show connections. A transcript tool and a zine both care about memory. A community graph and a mutual-aid service both force questions about trust and power.

Publishing those connections is a governance choice. Routes, status labels, project histories, and repository links tell visitors how the collective operates, and they are more revealing than a polished paragraph on an about page. The design carries the politics the same way. The zine makes room for arguments longer than a post. The archive keeps earlier work available. Repository links are public evidence. A newsletter signup sits in the footer, optional, with no tracking.

None of this produces perfect coherence. A collective contains disagreements, abandoned paths, and work at different stages. The site has to make that uneven record navigable without sanding it into a brand story.

The internet has plenty of launch pages. We wanted a place where an idea stays available after launch day, still attached to the work around it.

You can leave the feed and still find us.
