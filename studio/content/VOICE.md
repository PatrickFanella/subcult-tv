# Business name and public branding

The full proper business name is **Subculture-Collective**. Use **SUBCULT**, **subcult.tv** or **SUBCULT.TV** for public-facing studio branding. Use the full name where a business identity is required. Keep each product's own name and visual identity.

# Voice guide

One voice per product. This file governs public copy on subcult.tv, the product landings, brand packs, social drafts and support pages. Facts come from [the claims register](social/Product%20Claims%20Register.md); this guide only decides how they are said. Sample lines below show the voice. They are not approved copy, and a sample never licenses a claim the register does not support.

## What went wrong before

Every product used to sound like the same careful narrator. The repeated shapes were the problem more than any single word:

- A two-fragment headline: "Find the passage. Keep the context." "Read the field. Trace the evidence." "Every link. One board."
- An introduction that states a problem in one sentence and has the product solve it in the next.
- Three features, three steps and three highlight chips, each titled with an imperative verb.
- An audience line reading "For X, Y, and anyone who Z."
- A closing imperative that restates the headline.
- "In one place", "with the source attached", "worth keeping".

A page may use one of these when it is the best way to say the thing. Two products on the same site may not share one. Let the number of features, steps and questions follow the product: Kork has a long list, Prichal has one job.

## Rules every voice keeps

- Say what the product does, who it is for and how finished it is. Maturity limits (pre-alpha, private preview, in development, sample text) stay in every rewrite.
- No invented numbers, users, testimonials, urgency or guarantees.
- No "seamless", "powerful", "effortless", "unlock", "empower", "elevate", "streamline", "reimagine", "game-changing", "all-in-one", "take control", "say goodbye to".
- No "not just X, but Y". No rhetorical "The result?". No closing line that summarizes the page.
- Em dashes are rare. Exclamation marks belong to Kork and clpr, one at a time.
- Headings use sentence case unless the brand sets capitals in artwork.
- Humor is allowed where a voice below says so. A joke never replaces the sentence that explains the product.
- Copy about a change describes what a person meets: who runs into it, what was in their way, what is different now. A reader never saw the old version, so "X replaces Y with Z" tells them nothing. Words from the review pipeline (merged, PR, live check, deployment, verification) stay in private notes; say "not live yet" or "in development" once, where it matters to the reader.
- Internal documents (plans, runbooks, READMEs, evidence records) have no brand voice. Write them plainly.

## SUBCULT

**Who is talking:** the collective, first person plural, standing next to a poster it just pasted up.

**Sounds like:** a zine masthead. Declarative, a little confrontational about platforms, specific about the work. Short paragraphs. It names what it is against only when it can name what it built instead.

**Does:** say "we". Lists real jobs: clips, archives, mutual aid, the door at a show. Uses signal, relay, archive, ledger when they describe something real.

**Avoids:** startup cheer, mission statements, "community-driven", mythic vocabulary stacked three deep, any promise about the whole umbrella.

**Sample lines:**
- "We make software for people who run things without a budget: a clip channel, a tape archive, a food run, a Friday show."
- "Nine products. None of them wants your attention for longer than the job takes."
- "The code is public. Read it before you trust us."

## clpr

**Who is talking:** a regular in chat who clips everything and remembers which stream it was.

**Sounds like:** fast, lowercase-friendly, amused. Talks about clips the way viewers do: the bit, the fail, the one from last Tuesday. Fragments are fine. One exclamation mark per page at most.

**Does:** write the name lowercase in running copy on the site (`clpr`), CLPR in Studio documents and artwork labels. Uses "clip" as a verb. Gives examples that sound like real clip hunts without naming real streamers.

**Avoids:** "content", "discover", "curate your experience", "moments that matter", emote spam, Twitch slang older than the reader.

**Sample lines:**
- "You saw it live. Good luck finding it again."
- "Clips by creator, topic and tag. Playlists for the ones you keep sending people."
- "Somebody clipped it. It's probably in here."

## Patchwork

**Who is talking:** a neighbor who has done this before and is not in a hurry.

**Sounds like:** a note on a community board, written in full sentences. Warm without being sweet. Small, ordinary examples: a ride to an appointment, a spare crib, a hot meal. It tells you plainly that this is a demonstration and that nobody should arrange real help through it yet.

**Does:** use "you" and "your neighbors". Spells it "neighbors", as the app and subcult.tv both do. States the location rule exactly: requests show approximate areas; approved public resources can show their addresses.

**Avoids:** hero language, "empower communities", urgency, crisis imagery, protocol terms (PDS, DID, AT identity) in public copy, any promise that help will come.

**Sample lines:**
- "Someone two streets over needs a ride on Thursday. You have a car on Thursday."
- "Ask for what you need. Offer what you have. Nobody posts their address."
- "This is a demo. Please don't use it for a real request yet."

## HasanAra

**Who is talking:** the continuity announcer of the Piker Broadcasting Service, reading from a broadsheet.

**Sounds like:** public television with a straight face. Mock-solemn, a bit cheesy on purpose, never mean. Newspaper furniture is welcome: editions, the record, corrections. The joke is the formality; the facts underneath are exact.

**Does:** call the archive "the record". Treats timestamps as citations. Says outright that transcripts are machine-made and need checking against the recording before anyone quotes them.

**Avoids:** stan voice, political editorializing in product copy, claiming complete coverage or accuracy, jokes at the expense of people in the recordings.

**Sample lines:**
- "He said it on stream. The record shows when."
- "Search several years of broadcasts by phrase. Every result opens at the timestamp."
- "Corrections: the transcripts are automatic and sometimes wrong. Check the tape before you quote it."

## Rekolekt

**Who is talking:** the archivist who built the catalogue and knows where everything is.

**Sounds like:** a finding aid. Quiet, exact, slightly dry. Longer sentences than the other products, with the mechanism in them. It is software for people who maintain a collection, so it talks to them as peers and does not sell.

**Does:** name the parts: recording, transcript, timestamp, passage, source link. Says it is software you deploy, with HasanAra as the running example, and that it is not a hosted upload service.

**Avoids:** "unlock your content", AI enthusiasm, "your own identity" as a flourish, any hint of a sign-up flow that does not exist.

**Sample lines:**
- "A four-hour recording is a bad place to keep a sentence."
- "Rekolekt transcribes each recording with timestamps, indexes the text, and links every passage back to the second it was spoken."
- "Each archive runs under its own name. HasanAra is one."

## Subcult OS

**Who is talking:** the stage manager, on comms, forty minutes to doors.

**Sounds like:** a run sheet. Clipped, time-stamped, role-first. Dry humor of someone who has run a door with a paper list. Copy can be set as cues: "19:00 doors", "22:45 close-out".

**Does:** name the roles and the moments: load-in, doors, the list, close-out. Says it is in development and built around free ticket reservations.

**Avoids:** "event management platform", nightlife glamour, promises about paid ticketing, payouts, app stores or a production service.

**Sample lines:**
- "17:00 load-in. 19:00 doors. Everyone on the team is looking at the same sheet."
- "Check guests in by name or code, on a phone, with one hand."
- "When the night ends you get a report, not a pile of screenshots."

## Undertow

**Who is talking:** the friend who makes visuals for everyone's releases and has opinions about codecs.

**Sounds like:** liner notes crossed with a plugin changelog. Sensory in the headline, numeric in the body: layer names, formats, resolutions. It can be loud for one line and then gets specific.

**Does:** name the actual parts: Milkdrop, spectrum, VU meters, LRC, SRT and VTT lyrics, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, H.264 MP4. Says rendering happens in the browser and that performance depends on the device.

**Avoids:** "bring your music to life", "stunning visuals", guaranteed speed, purchasable cloud plans (live billing is off).

**Sample lines:**
- "The track is done. Now it needs something to look at."
- "Six templates if you're in a hurry. Every layer is yours to move if you're not."
- "It renders on your machine. Your audio doesn't leave it."

## Prichal

**Who is talking:** ground control, in plain words.

**Sounds like:** a calm operations desk. Procedural and reassuring, with short declaratives and no jargon. The docking idea lives in two words, dock and port, and nowhere else. It explains what a server is to someone who has never needed to know.

**Does:** keep the approved lines "Your server. We keep it running." and "Dock your account. We handle the rest." Says posts are public wherever the server runs, and that this is a private preview with no sign-up.

**Avoids:** explaining or correcting the pronunciation, marketing leaving, privacy or censorship resistance, protocol acronyms without a plain gloss, space cosplay ("liftoff", "mission").

**Sample lines:**
- "Every account on the open social web lives on a server somewhere. This one is yours, and we do the maintenance."
- "You keep posting from Bluesky. Nothing about that changes."
- "Status: private preview. We are still testing backups and recovery, so the door is closed for now."

## Kork

**Who is talking:** whoever keeps the corkboard by the venue door tidy, and enjoys it.

**Sounds like:** a hand-lettered flyer. Chummy and a little corny on purpose. One pin pun per page is the budget, and it should be a good one. Lists are long because the product's list is long, so let them run.

**Does:** lead with the plain offer: a free link page, your payment buttons go to your own accounts, no cut. Gives the real numbers (92 services, ten themes). States the 24-hour wait before a new account can publish.

**Avoids:** "link in bio tool", creator-economy talk, custom domains, paid plans or prices, user counts, a second pun.

**Sample lines:**
- "One link in your bio. Everything else pinned to it."
- "Tips go to your Cash App, not ours. We don't take a cut because we never touch the money."
- "It's free, it's new, and it will have rough edges. Tell us where."

## Left Field

**Who is talking:** the research desk, with the footnotes still attached.

**Sounds like:** a methods section written by someone who can also write a headline. Limits come before conclusions. Dry, occasionally wry in a heading ("Where the field bends."), never in a number.

**Does:** say "provisional", "versioned", "not in this score", "unavailable". Works at seat level with public records. Names what is missing.

**Avoids:** prediction, horse-race language, "data-driven", "insights", anything implying an endorsement or voter-level targeting.

**Sample lines:**
- "A score here is a research note with a version number. It is not a forecast."
- "Where we have no record, the page says so. It does not show a zero."
- "Start with a seat. Then read what we couldn't find."

## Dedhed

**Who is talking:** a taper labelling spines at the kitchen table.

**Sounds like:** J-card handwriting. Dates first, venue second, source notes in shorthand a head would recognize. Affectionate and unfussy. A little corny is fine; it is a tape collection.

**Does:** write shows the way tapers do. Keeps uncertain set boundaries and credits visible. States that it is a private pilot with no public address.

**Avoids:** quoting lyrics, tie-dye clichés, "long strange trip", any suggestion of official endorsement or a public station.

**Sample lines:**
- "Pull a tape off the shelf, or tune in to whatever everyone else is hearing."
- "Set II starts somewhere in here. The label says where we think."

## Tadooer

**Who is talking:** the planner itself, quietly.

**Sounds like:** one short sentence at a time, present tense. No productivity sermon. It is a private tool for one owner, so the copy never recruits. All promotional copy in the pack is sample text until the owner reviews it; keep that label.

**Avoids:** "get more done", "supercharge", streaks, hustle, a URL, a sign-up invitation.

**Sample lines:**
- "Here is the day. Here is the one thing you're doing now."
- "The rest can wait in the list."

## Patrick__Eff

**Who is talking:** Patrick, first person singular.

**Sounds like:** a working note from someone who writes software and makes music and does not separate the two. Plain, a bit self-deprecating, specific about tools. "Software. Sound. Culture." stays as the identity line.

**Avoids:** founder voice, third-person bio, "passionate about".

## Chatty Cat

**Who is talking:** the person with six chats open who still catches your message.

**Sounds like:** quick, practical, a little dry. Chat vocabulary used correctly: rooms, the log, scrollback, backfill, a reconnect. Short sentences, lowercase tolerated in chat-facing copy, no hype. It is a viewer's tool and talks like a viewer who reads fast.

**Does:** say what it is in one breath: a self-hosted Twitch chat client that merges up to six channels and keeps your display settings. Names the lineage (a rebuild of mxmilkiib's HasanAbi Chat Thing, AGPL) and the state (in migration; the original app stays up until parity). Describes settings as the user's choices, not features it bestows. Writes the name as Chatty Cat in prose and `chatty_cat` only in the wordmark; lowercase headlines are fine in artwork.

**Avoids:** "community", stan voice, HasanAra's mock-formality, clpr's exclamation marks, any claim about Twitch moderation or whispers that a release gate has not passed, and any suggestion that it is a Twitch product.

**Sample lines:**
- "All six channels in one scrollback."
- "Your palette, your font, your line height. It remembers."
- "It is the old Chat Thing, rebuilt so we can host it. Same log, same settings, our server. New name, same cat."

## Support and Patreon

**Who is talking:** the workshop, showing its ledger.

**Sounds like:** a frank note about money. Says what support pays for and what it does not buy. First person plural. No gratitude theatre and no tiers described as experiences.

**Sample lines:**
- "Paying doesn't unlock the software. It stays public either way."
- "A paid membership covers hosting, tools and hours, and gets you into the private Discord channels and the supporter posts. We post when we have something to share, not on a schedule."

## Teasers and The Common House

Fiction voice. Write from inside the world, in the present tense, and stop early. No release dates, mechanics or character names that the design notes have not fixed.
