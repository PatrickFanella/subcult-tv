# Moderation is infrastructure

We tend to notice moderation only after something has gone wrong. A rule appears, someone gets banned, or a thread closes. By then, the harder work has already been missed.

Moderation belongs in the design of a community from the start.

Every system assumes people will behave in certain ways. Some leave openings for abuse. Others put so many locks on participation that nobody wants to stay. Good moderation plans for conflict without letting conflict run the place.

---

Coordinated disruption can look boring at first. Maybe it is a burst of junk submissions, a cluster of votes arriving together, or someone testing the same boundary over and over.

Each action is easy to dismiss. The pattern is what damages trust.

Waiting for a volunteer to catch every incident is a bad system. People get tired. Similar cases get different outcomes. The loudest problem takes the day while quieter damage piles up.

Rate limits can slow coordinated manipulation. Trust built through participation can affect how much weight an action carries. Public decision logs give people something firmer than rumor. Each measure helps keep a shared space usable.

---

This still requires judgment. Rules cannot anticipate every conflict, and unlimited discretion makes enforcement feel personal. Clear norms give moderators a basis for acting and members a basis for questioning a decision.

When this work goes well, it can be hard to see. That does not mean the community runs itself. Someone wrote the rules, kept records, noticed patterns, and made room for an appeal.

Moderation decisions show what a community will protect and what it will tolerate. For SUBCULT, those choices matter at least as much as another feature release.

Arbitrary enforcement teaches people to avoid the moderator rather than trust the process. Written reasons and visible principles make decisions open to scrutiny. Shared procedures also keep the whole job off one person's shoulders.

> Care can be built into the system.

Treating moderation as an afterthought leaves people to absorb preventable harm. Designing it into the project gives disagreement somewhere to go besides a pile-on, a private feud, or an exhausted volunteer's inbox.

That is the kind of openness we want: maintained, accountable, and able to last.
