# The feed without the feed: reclaiming discovery

We started clpr after failing to find things we knew we had seen.

The usual case was a live stream. A clip caught a reaction, a narrow victory, or an unexpected confession. It circulated for a day and then went under newer posts. Finding it later meant remembering an exact title or the account that posted it, and often we remembered neither. The feed still had plenty to show us, just not the thing we wanted.

That irritation shaped the product. On clpr you look for clips by creator, topic, and tag, and you put the ones you keep into playlists you can share. Nothing waits for a feed to bring it back.

Feeds are good at replacement. Each new item pushes the last one farther away, and infinite scroll makes that feel effortless, which suits a business that wants another minute of attention. Search starts from the other end. The person chooses the subject and can revise the query when the results miss.

Many products count time on site as success. We would rather someone find the clip and leave. Some visits do run longer, because someone is building a collection or tracing related moments, and that is fine. Duration only means something next to what the person came to do. So clpr puts browsing, playlists, and sharing ahead of autoplay, and none of those needs an endless session.

The screen is quieter as a result, and it may feel sparse to someone used to constant prompts. Good. The empty space is where you decide what comes next.

An archive only matters when people can get things back out of it. With a catalog, a community can recover overlooked contributions and keep its clips on its own terms, with less reason to trust whatever an opaque ranking system surfaces today.

clpr is our working case that a platform can help people find and remember things without treating their attention as raw material.
